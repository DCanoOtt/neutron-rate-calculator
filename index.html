Neutron Reaction Rate Calculator

Instructions

1. What the page does

It folds the neutron fluence of a facility with a pointwise (continuous-energy) cross section from an ACE library. The result is the reaction rate of the sample as a function of neutron energy, its integral, and how that integral divides over energy decades. All rates are time-averaged and given in 1/s unless "per pulse" is stated.

2. Quick start

(1) Load the cross sections: press Choose folder and select the folder that holds xsdir, or load one or more ACE files. (2) Choose the beam line, and change repetition rate and flight path if needed. (3) Find the isotope and tick one or more reaction channels. (4) Enter the sample: mass or number of atoms, beam spot radius, and density if you want a thickness. (5) Optionally apply the time-of-flight resolution function. (6) Read the three charts, the summary band under them and the tables further down. Everything recalculates as soon as a field changes. Use the Axis toggle above the charts to show energy or time of flight. For a compound, add the other isotopes under Compound before reading the results.

3. Left panel

Facility. The beam lines come from the fluence file (title, number of points, then energy–flux pairs; energy in MeV, flux in n/(cm²·s·ΔlnE)). The file read at start is Fluxes01.txt. Fluence file for all beam lines replaces the whole set with another file of the same layout. Replace this beam line's fluence pastes or loads a two-column table for the selected line only; choose its energy unit and whether the values are per ΔlnE, per MeV or per pulse. Repetition rate and Flight path start at the nominal values of the line; Reset to nominal restores them. The information list shows the pulse spacing, the fluence scale against the nominal setting and the pulse-overlap energy. The title of n_TOF-EAR2 says 20 m, but its real flight path is 19.55 m, and the page uses 19.55 m as the nominal value for it (in the flight path field, the fluence scaling and the time of flight).

Resolution (time of flight), in the Facility group. Switches the resolution function on. Pulse width starts at the value of the selected beam line (n_TOF: σ = 7 ns, shown as 16.484 ns FWHM; NFS 5.6 ns, DONES 5.6 ns, GELINA 1 ns); you can overwrite it, and it is remembered for each beam line until Reset to nominal. Added width adds other contributions (default 0). The select chooses whether those two widths are FWHM or σ. Target / moderator spread adds the contribution of the production target and moderator. Monte Carlo RF (default for EAR1, EAR2 and NEAR) replaces the target spread by a resolution function. The EAR1 and EAR2 functions come from Monte Carlo histograms, parameterised with smoothed spline parameters so that statistical spikes and valleys of the histograms do not appear. The NEAR (6 m) function was not simulated: it was extrapolated from the EAR1 and EAR2 target-only functions (quantiles linear in ln L, with no extrapolation below 0.3 eV where the thermal shapes differ) and refitted, so treat it as indicative. For EAR1 and NEAR the proton pulse (σ = 7 ns, shown as FWHM) is added by the page. For EAR2 (19.55 m) it already contains the proton pulse (7 ns RMS) and the target and moderator, so the pulse width and ΔL fields are disabled and only Additional width still applies. For EAR1 (185 m, FLUKA) it contains the target and moderator only: the proton pulse is added as a Gaussian in time, with a default of 7 ns RMS (16.48 ns FWHM, shown in the pulse field in the units chosen below) that you can change. The flight path of the resolution function is used for the time-of-flight conversion and axis. The resolution function gives the distribution of the extra equivalent path length λ for each neutron energy, as a Gaussian convolved with two exponential tails, (1 − f2) EMG(μ, σ, τ1) + f2 EMG(μ, σ, τ2), with the parameters (EAR2: μ about 6 cm at low energy and 30 cm above 1 MeV; fast tail τ1 of 2 to 40 cm, slow tail τ2 of 40 to 130 cm; EAR1: edge at about 10 cm) interpolated in log E; for EAR1 below about 0.1 eV a third broad Gaussian around 150 cm describes thermal neutrons that leave the moderator late; a neutron of energy E arrives at t = (L + λ) / v. Each energy bin of the rate spectrum is spread over apparent times with this distribution, so the total rate is conserved. The distribution has a long tail towards later times (lower apparent energies) and, above about 10 MeV, towards negative λ. Fixed path length ΔL (default 0.5 cm for every beam line without a Monte Carlo RF) gives a time spread ΔL / v(E). The total resolution is the quadrature sum of the pulse width, the additional width (detector, electronics, moderator) and the fixed-path-length target spread; the line under the fields gives the resulting ΔE/E at 1 eV, 1 keV and 1 MeV. The total resolution is the quadrature sum of all parts. The flight path is the one in the beam line name. See section 5 for what is broadened.

Cross sections (ACE). A browser cannot open a path that is typed in. The path field is only a reminder (it is kept in this browser). The folder button opens a file dialog (on macOS press ⌘⇧G and paste the path). The page then looks for xsdir, reads the list of ASCII neutron tables (names such as 26056.80c) and finds each isotope's file by the name written in xsdir, in any subfolder of the chosen folder (a datapath= line in xsdir, or a folder in the file name, narrows the search when several files share a name). Only the isotope you pick is read, so a large library loads quickly. Without an xsdir, the page reads the headers of all ACE files in the folder. Single ACE files can be loaded with the second chooser. Type-1 ASCII files are supported, with the old 12-line header and the ACE 2.0.0 header; binary ACE is not. The status line below the buttons reports what was read, or the file name and the problem when something fails.

Find isotope filters the list: use Au-197, 79197 or a full ZAID such as 26056.80c. If nothing matches, or the xsdir entry points to a file that is not in the folder, an error message says so.

Reaction channels. The list holds the MTR/SIG reactions of the table, plus total (MT 1), elastic (MT 2) and absorption (MT 101) from the main energy grid. MT 3 (non-elastic) is derived as total minus elastic. Tick one or more. At least one stays ticked. Reset reaction selection returns to a single reaction: radiative capture (MT 102), or fission (MT 18) if the table has no capture channel, and puts the result selectors back to their defaults.

Compound (optional). The isotope chosen above is the primary component. Add component adds another isotope with its number of atoms per primary atom (real values allowed, for example 2 for the oxygen in UO₂). Each component has its own isotope filter, table selector and list of reactions to count, where you can tick several. Reset reaction selection unticks them all and Remove deletes the component. A component with no reaction ticked only takes part in the self-shielding; with reactions ticked, each one gets its own row in Rates by reaction. Loading another library clears the components.

Sample and energy window. Sample mass (mg) and Number of atoms N are linked: N is the number of atoms of the primary isotope (it can be real, for example 1.5e18), and the mass is the total mass of all components. The atoms of the other components are r × N, where r is the ratio you entered, and they are listed in Rates by reaction. N follows from the mass as N = mass / (AWR × 1.00866 u / NA) for a single isotope, and with the summed atomic masses of all components for a compound. Thickness, Density and Beam spot radius are also linked: the spot is taken as a circle of area A = π r², and thickness = mass / (density × A). Editing the thickness changes the mass. Density starts at the density of the element (a table inside the page); you can overwrite it, and Use element density restores the default. For a compound there is no default, so enter the density of the mixture. E min and E max set the energy window of the integral. Include self-shielding switches the correction on or off (see 7).

4. Main panel

Summary band. Rate per target atom (and the thin-sample value when self-shielding is on), rate in the sample, and reactions per beam pulse, for the reaction chosen in Results for.

Results for. This selector above the charts lists the ticked reactions of every component, grouped by isotope. An isotope with two or more ticked reactions also has Σ selected reactions, the sum of its ticked reactions. The choice drives the summary band, the cumulative curve, the per-atom curves and the facility comparison. For an extra isotope, the in-sample and per-pulse values use that isotope's number of atoms (its ratio × N).

Whole compound. With two or more isotopes that have reactions ticked, Results for also offers Σ over isotopes, weighted by atoms. The charts then show, for each isotope, the sum of its ticked reactions (the individual reaction curves are left out) multiplied by its atoms per primary atom, and the compound total as the main curve. The cross section is then in barn per primary atom, and the rate density is per primary atom or per sample, as set by the toggle. The summary band, the cumulative curve, the decade table and the facility comparison use the compound total. The self-shielding of the sample is already common to all isotopes.

Sum of reactions. Σ is built as one cross section σΣ(E) = Σ σk(E) on the union of the energy grids of its reactions, and is then folded with the fluence and the shielding like any other reaction. Because the shielding factor depends only on the total cross section, the rate density of the sum is exactly the sum of the individual rate densities. Overlapping channels (MT 101 with MT 102, MT 3 with MT 2 or MT 4, MT 1 with anything) are counted twice, so tick only channels that do not overlap.

Reaction rate or yield. Third plot swaps the third panel between the reaction rate density and the reaction yield, the number of reactions per incident neutron: Y(E) = (N / A) σeff(E), with N the number of atoms of the chosen isotope in the sample, A the beam spot area and σeff = rate density per atom / fluence (self-shielding and the resolution function included). For a thin sample this is (N / A) σ(E); for a thick one it tends to (1 − e−nσtot) σ / σtot. The cumulative curve and the per-atom / per-sample choice only apply to the rate. Saved plots and ROOT histograms follow the choice (the histogram is called yield).

Isotope chart. The Isotope chart button above Find isotope opens a floating chart of the nuclides (neutron number N across, proton number Z up). Colours give the main decay mode of the ground state: black stable, blue β⁻, light red EC / β⁺, yellow α, green spontaneous fission, orange proton emission, violet neutron emission, tan other. Isotopes that have a table in the loaded library are drawn in full colour with an outline; the others are faded. Use the wheel or the + / − buttons to zoom, or drag a rectangle (Zoom box) to zoom into a region; Move (or Shift+drag) drags the chart; double-click or Whole chart shows everything, and Selected centres on the current isotope. The title bar moves the window and its corner resizes it. The magic numbers 2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82 and 126 are bracketed by thin dashed orange lines and labelled in orange on both axes. A click on an isotope with data selects it as the isotope of the page; Shift+click adds it to the compound. Moving the pointer over an isotope opens a small window with its name, Z, N, A, decay mode, half-life and the ZAIDs available. Decay data: NUBASE.

Saving the plots. The Save plot button (next to Reset zoom) opens a menu with PNG, PDF, ASCII and ROOT histograms. PNG and PDF save the three panels as they are shown, with a white background (the PDF is one page that holds the picture). ASCII writes one text table with a commented header (run description and a numbered list of columns): energy, fluence, and for the cross section and the rate density the main curve, the sum of the selected reactions and every individual reaction (MT) of every component, with the compound total if there is one. Curves with another energy grid are linearly interpolated onto the grid of column 1. The normalisation is that of the plots: per atom or in the sample, or reactions per incident neutron for the yield. ROOT histograms writes one ROOT file with the curves of the three panels as TH1D histograms on the energy range shown (including the zoom): phi, sigma, rate, plus rate_thin_sample, rate_without_resolution, rate_cumulative_fraction and, when several reactions or components are selected, one histogram for every individual reaction and for each sum (names such as sigma_n_g_MT102, rate_n_2n_MT16, rate_sum_selected; the first histograms sigma and rate are the curve shown as main curve, usually the sum). Everything selected is written, whichever curve is displayed. Each bin spans the geometric mid-points between neighbouring energy points of the table, in MeV, and its content is the value at that point (the rate density is per unit ln E). The file is delivered inside a zip because the viewer only allows some file types. You are asked to confirm the file name.

Charts. Three panels share one logarithmic energy axis: the fluence Φ(E), the cross section σ(E), and the rate density (rate per unit ln E). The energy values are written under every panel and, when you zoom into a narrow window, switch to round numbers on a linear scale so there is always a reference. Each panel has its title and units above it and, where needed, a colour legend in a band above the frame, so nothing is written over the curves. When several reactions are drawn, every ticked reaction of the chosen isotope has its own colour in the σ and rate-density panels (with Σ drawn first when it is the chosen result), and the cumulative curve belongs to the chosen result. Linear / Log sets the vertical axis of all three panels (fluence, cross section and rate density; log by default); on log axes the range grows up to 18 decades when several curves are drawn. Per atom / Per sample chooses whether the rate density is for one atom or for the whole sample (the default is the whole sample). The dashed black line is the cumulative rate in % (right axis); the dotted line is the thin-sample value, shown for a single curve when self-shielding is on. The shaded band marks energies below the pulse-overlap energy. The Axis toggle switches the horizontal axis between neutron energy and time of flight (see section 5). Move the pointer over the charts to read E, t and the values there; drag across the charts to zoom in; double-click or press Reset zoom to return to the full window.

Zooming. Drag across the plots to zoom the energy range only (the vertical axes stay as they are). Hold Shift while dragging to draw a box inside one plot: the horizontal range of all plots follows the width of the box and the vertical range of that plot follows its height. A Shift-drag that is nearly horizontal or nearly vertical (under 6 pixels in the other direction) changes only one axis. A plot with a manual vertical range shows [y zoomed] after its title; the other plots stay on autoscale, and the rate and the yield plots remember their ranges separately. Double-click a plot or press Reset zoom to return to the full energy range and autoscale in all plots. Save plot as PNG or PDF stores what is on screen, while ASCII and ROOT files hold the whole data.

Rate by energy decade. The share of the total rate in each decade of neutron energy, in %, in 1/s for the sample and per pulse. Its own Reaction selector, next to the title, offers the same list as Results for. Same as results (the default) follows that selector; any other choice keeps the decade table on that reaction or sum while the rest of the page shows something else.

All facilities, same isotope and reaction. The same calculation for every beam line, with the reaction chosen in Results for. Each line is at its nominal rate and path (your own settings for the selected line). The columns are the rate per atom, in the sample and per pulse. Relative is a bar for the per-atom rate on a logarithmic scale: the best facility is a full bar, and each factor of 10 below it shortens the bar by one sixth (minimum 2 %). Read the exact ratios from the numbers.

Rates by reaction. Shown when more than one reaction or component is listed. It has one row for every ticked reaction of every component, with the atoms, the rate per atom, in the sample and per pulse. A component with no reaction ticked is listed as shielding only. The last row sums the listed reactions; overlapping channels are counted twice there.

5. Resolution and time of flight

Resolution (time of flight). Tick Apply resolution function. The resolution width is the quadrature sum σ = √(σpulse² + σadd² + σtarget(E)²) of the particle pulse width of the beam line (with its default value), an additional width and the energy-dependent target / moderator spread (a fixed path length ΔL, or the Monte Carlo resolution function). The pulse and additional widths are entered as FWHM (σ = FWHM / 2.3548) or as σ; the target spread is a FWHM. The flight path is the one in the name of the beam line, in metres (for example 185 m for n_TOF-EAR1), not the value in the Flight path field. The hint below the field gives the corresponding energy resolution ΔE/E at 1 eV, 1 keV and 1 MeV for that beam line.

Every energy is converted to a time of flight with the relativistic relation t = L / (β c), β = √(γ² − 1) / γ, γ = 1 + E / (mnc²), with mnc² = 939.565 MeV. The resolution is a Gaussian in t whose width depends on the neutron energy: σt(E)² = σpulse² + σadd² + σtarget(E)². The target part is a fixed path length, σtarget = ΔL / v(E) / 2.3548 (or the Monte Carlo resolution function, see above). For a fixed path length ΔE/E ≈ 2ΔL/L does not depend on energy. The cross section is not broadened: it stays the evaluated σ(E), because the resolution belongs to the measurement and not to the nucleus. Only the rate spectrum is blurred. The rate spectrum (after self-shielding) is treated as a count rate per ns, R(t) = ρ(E) / |dt/dlnE|. Each true energy spreads its counts with its own width, and the result is converted back, so the integral over the window is conserved apart from the window edges. This is the order in which a real measurement works: the sample shields with the true cross section and the detector then blurs the spectrum. The fluence is not broadened. With a single curve and the resolution on, the rate-density panel also draws the unresolved curve as a grey dashed line (without resolution), so you can see what the resolution does. The total rate is conserved by construction, so the numbers in the summary change very little; the effect is in the shape of narrow structures. The summary, the decade table, the cumulative curve and the facility comparison (each beam line with its own flight path) all use the resolved spectra. For widths smaller than the energy grid spacing nothing changes.

Axis. The Axis toggle above the charts switches the horizontal axis between neutron energy and time of flight (logarithmic, with the shortest times on the left, so high energies are still on the left). Zooming, the pointer readout (which shows both E and t) and the pulse-overlap shading work in both modes.

6. How the rate is calculated

For one atom, R = ∫ σ(E) Φ(E) d lnE, with σ in barn (1 b = 10⁻²⁴ cm²) and Φ in n/(cm²·s) per unit ln E. The sample rate is R × (number of atoms), and the rate per pulse is that value divided by the repetition rate.

The integration grid is the union of the ACE energies and the fluence energies inside the window, refined so that no step exceeds Δ ln E = 0.03 (3 %). The cross section is interpolated linearly in energy (the ACE convention), Φ log–log (linear if it contains zeros), and the integral is a trapezoid sum in ln E. The cumulative curve, the decade shares and the total all come from the same sum.

Changing the setup scales the fluence: Φ ∝ (f / f₀) (L₀ / L)², where f₀ and L₀ are the nominal values. The fluence is time-averaged at the nominal rate, so "1/s" means reactions per second of beam time for every facility. The instantaneous rate during a pulse is higher by the inverse duty cycle. The 1/L² law assumes a point-like source seen from far away.

Sample and atoms. The number of atoms in the beam is N = mass / (AWR × 1.00866 u). The sample is a uniform slab of areal density n = N / (π r²), uniformly illuminated by the beam. The density only converts between mass and thickness (thickness = mass / (density × area)); it starts at the density of the element of the selected isotope and you can overwrite it. Editing the thickness changes the mass.

Compound. The isotope in the Cross sections group is the primary component and N is its number of atoms. Each extra component i has ri atoms per primary atom, so its areal density is ri n. The shielding weight uses τ = Σi ri n σtot,i(E), summed over all components, so a scatterer such as oxygen shields the resonances of uranium. The sample mass is the total mass N Σ ri Ai u. The element density no longer applies, so enter the density of the mixture to get a thickness.

ACE input. ASCII type-1 continuous-energy neutron tables, located through the xsdir. Isotopes are identified by their ZAID string (for example 26056.80c). Reactions come from the MTR/SIG blocks, plus total (MT 1), elastic (MT 2) and absorption (MT 101) from the main grid. MT 3 is derived as total minus elastic. The cross section is never broadened by the program.

Pulse overlap: neutrons slower than E = ½ mn (L f / c)² arrive after the next pulse has started, so below that energy the spectrum mixes several pulses.

7. Self-shielding

A strong resonance absorbs most of the beam in the first layers, so the real rate is lower than the thin-sample value. The page treats the sample as a uniform slab of areal density n = N / area (atoms per barn) and multiplies the integrand by (1 − e−τ) / τ, with τ(E) = Σi ri n σtot,i(E). The sum runs over the primary isotope (r = 1) and all components, using MT 1 of each table. This is the first-collision attenuation of the beam. The "peak optical depth" is the largest τ in the window; the thin-sample percentage shows how much the correction lowers the total.

Self-shielding model. Three models are available in the sample section. (1) Simple: first collision is the factor above; a neutron that scatters is lost. (2) Second collision (analytic) adds the neutrons that scatter once and then react: for an infinite slab, normal incidence and isotropic scattering without energy change, Y2 = (Σel/Σt)(Σx/Σt) G(T), with G(T) = ∫0T e−t[1 − E2(t)/2 − E2(T−t)/2] dt, T = n Σt, and Σel from MT 2. (3) Multiple scattering (Monte Carlo) follows the neutrons after the first collision (the first collision itself is exact and analytic): elastic scattering, isotropic in the centre of mass, with the energy change, a free-gas target below 400 kT at the temperature of the table, and every other reaction removing the neutron. It runs in the background on a grid of energies refined where the total cross section changes (histories per energy are set in the box; 2000 is a good start), and the plots update when it is ready. For thin samples the three models agree. They differ at strong resonances of thick samples, and the Monte Carlo also follows the energy change in a scattering, which matters when the energy loss is comparable to the resonance width. The yield and the rate saved in the files and the plots use the chosen model.

Limits of the models: the simple model treats scattered neutrons as removed (no scatter-in), so it is a lower bound of the rate for scattering-dominated samples. The correction models assume an infinite slab of the areal density N / area (no lateral leakage), elastic scattering from MT 2 only (no inelastic scatter-in, no angular distributions from the ACE file), no delay of the scattered neutron in the time of flight, and no sample holder or background. The beam is assumed uniform over a circular spot of area π r². There is no Doppler or sample-temperature treatment beyond the temperature of the table you choose, and unresolved-resonance probability tables are not used. Cross sections are those of the pointwise ACE table.

8. Tips and checks

If the rate looks too low in a resonance, lower the mass or enlarge the spot radius, and compare with the dotted thin-sample line. A very small E min needs a fluence table that reaches it; outside the table the fluence is not extrapolated, so the rate there is zero. Check the status line below the folder button when a library does not load: it reports the file name and the problem. The resolution only changes structures that are not much wider than it. In the resonance region, check the ΔE/E in the hint against the resonance widths: a resolution much narrower than a resonance leaves it unchanged, while a much wider one flattens it. In time-of-flight mode the dashed cumulative curve still accumulates in energy, so it falls from left to right. If an extra isotope shows no rate, make sure at least one reaction is ticked for it; with none ticked it only shields.
